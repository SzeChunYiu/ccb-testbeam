# Same-particle timing, pile-up, and particle identification in a two-arm scintillator range telescope: a reproduce-first analysis with adversarial Monte-Carlo validation

**CCB analysis collaboration**

*Status: preliminary research manuscript, not peer-reviewed. All figures are produced from the Python analysis chain described in Methods; this document is the text draft. Every quantitative claim traces to an internal report listed in Appendix A.*

---

## Abstract

Scintillator range telescopes are workhorse detectors for beam diagnostics and low-energy nuclear physics, but their achievable timing precision and their tolerance to beam pile-up are set by the pulse waveform, which is easy to over-model. Here we characterise both, using data from a test beam at the Cyclotron Centre Bronowice (CCB, Kraków) for the HIBEAM–NNBAR programme, in which 190 MeV protons struck a deuterated-polyethylene (CD₂) target and the scattered charged particles were recorded by two independent HRD scintillator range-telescope arms at conjugate angles, each behind its own trigger scintillators. Working from raw waveforms under a reproduce-first discipline, with three leakage controls and a GEANT4 Monte-Carlo (MC) truth bridge, we find that the pulse is low-dimensional in shape and well described by analytic models: an analytic amplitude–timewalk correction reaches a same-particle pair timing resolution of σ₆₈ ≈ 1.49–1.55 ns and is hard to beat, per-pair resolution scales as 1/A (better than 1/√A) in the high-lever stave pairs, and the downstream per-stave resolution reaches ≈0.85–1.1 ns at high amplitude. We revise the pile-up rate ceiling from a previously quoted 4.2 MHz to a data-driven one-sided bound R_max ≤ 3.05 MHz (censoring-aware estimators suggest ≈2.1 MHz or lower), and report the first independent MC live-time, which disagrees with data by +8% (134.99 vs 124.79 ns) — an honest disagreement, not a closure. Proton/deuteron identification is MC-closed at AUC = 0.986, with data methods within 0.5% of that ceiling; the coincidence trigger enriches Sample I in deuterons, confirmed at truth level (B2 deuteron-fraction ratio I/II = 1.519) and in data (B2 high-amplitude fraction ratio 3.45). A large MC–data stopping-depth discrepancy (χ²/ndf = 68,269) is traced not to missing detector material but to the unsimulated two-arm coincidence trigger: a truth-level trigger proxy plus a trigger-consistent gain collapses it by 109×. Machine learning helps decisively only where the missing information genuinely lives in waveform shape (saturation recovery, duplicate-readout closure); it ties or loses where an analytic physics model is already optimal, and several apparent wins were rejected by leakage controls. This work is as much a case study in disciplined, adversarially validated detector analysis — its headline numbers were corrected by an external review midway — as it is a measurement.

## 1. Introduction

Plastic-scintillator range telescopes convert a stopping charged particle into a short light pulse that is read out as a sampled waveform. Two quantities determine how useful such a detector is at a high-rate beam: how precisely a hit can be timestamped, and how high the beam rate can climb before overlapping pulses (pile-up) corrupt the measurement. Both are properties of the pulse waveform and of the readout chain, and both are easy to mis-estimate — timing because the correction for amplitude-dependent walk is subtle, and pile-up because the effective dead-time window is censored by the finite acquisition window. The temptation, increasingly, is to replace the analytic treatment of these effects with a learned model; the honest question is whether that helps, and where.

The measurement setting here is a test beam for the HIBEAM–NNBAR programme, run at the CCB cyclotron in Kraków. A 190 MeV proton beam struck a CD₂ target; proton–deuteron (pd) elastic scattering sends the scattered proton into one detector arm and the kinematically correlated recoil deuteron into the other. The two arms — labelled Stack A and Stack B — are independent HRD scintillator range telescopes at conjugate angles, roughly 100 cm from the target, each sitting behind its own pair of trigger scintillators, with a time-projection chamber (TPC) in front of Stack A only. Stack B, the primary analysis object, is an eight-bar range stack read out on its even physical staves (B2, B4, B6, B8); each bar returns an 18-sample, 10-ns-spaced (180 ns) waveform through a wavelength-shifting fibre and SiPM. The two trigger configurations define the two data samples used throughout: Sample I requires an A·B coincidence, Sample II requires the B trigger only.

The gap this work addresses is not a single unmeasured number but a methodology: on a single beam dataset with no per-event truth, how does one establish timing resolution, a pile-up ceiling, and particle identification (PID) that a reviewer will believe — and how does one decide honestly whether machine learning (ML) adds anything over strong conventional methods? Our answer couples three disciplines. First, reproduce-first: every study re-derives its inputs from the raw ROOT files and passes an exact count gate before any claim is made. Second, leakage controls: every ML-vs-traditional comparison is run on held-out beam runs and stress-tested with target-shuffle, leave-one-run-out (LORO), and event-block-shuffle sentinels, because a label that is a disguised function of the input will otherwise manufacture a spurious win. Third, a GEANT4 MC truth bridge (validation studies MV0–MV7), used adversarially — MC is asked to falsify data claims, and MC failures are reported rather than papered over.

This manuscript should be read with one unusual feature in view. Midway through the programme an independent seven-track external review found that the MC-validation layer, several headline numbers, and a set of claimed "gap closures" were unsound — some results had been written into report directories without any implementing code. We take the corrected state, after a full post-review program (retractions, cheap reruns, and targeted physics upgrades) completed in July 2026, as the version of record. Where a 2026-06 claim and its post-review replacement disagree, the replacement stands, and we say so explicitly. The result is a manuscript whose most transferable contribution may be the demonstration that an autonomous, high-throughput analysis program can be made trustworthy only by adversarial validation and honest retraction — not by volume of studies.

We report five physics-facing findings and one methodological one. (i) Timing is analytic-limited: an amplitude-only timewalk correction reaches σ₆₈ ≈ 1.49–1.55 ns and the per-pair resolution follows a 1/A law. (ii) The pile-up ceiling is R_max ≤ 3.05 MHz as a one-sided bound, and the first independent MC live-time disagrees with data by +8%. (iii) Proton/deuteron PID is MC-closed at AUC = 0.986. (iv) The coincidence trigger enriches Sample I in deuterons, confirmed in both truth and data. (v) The large MC–data stopping-depth discrepancy is caused by the unsimulated coincidence trigger, not missing material, and (vi) the ML-vs-traditional verdict is a split decision that we report task by task.

## 2. Apparatus and data

**Beam and target.** A proton beam of kinetic energy 190 MeV was incident on a CD₂ (deuterated polyethylene) target. The physics channel exploited throughout is pd-elastic scattering, which produces a two-body final state: at the Stack-B angle (≈38°) the recoil deuteron carries ≈105 MeV and the scattered proton ≈153 MeV, so the two species stop at different depths in the range stack — the deuteron near the B2/B4 boundary and the proton near B8 (GEANT4 CSDA ranges in the scintillator, phase2_geometry).

**Two independent arms.** Stack A and Stack B are separate HRD scintillator range telescopes placed at conjugate angles, each ≈100 cm from the target and each behind its own pair of trigger scintillator paddles; a proto-TPC sits in front of Stack A only. Because the arms view conjugate angles, an A·B coincidence tags a correlated pd-elastic **pair** — a proton in one arm and its partner deuteron in the other — never the same particle twice. This is the physical basis for the sample definitions and for the deuteron enrichment reported in §5.3–5.4. Stack B is the primary analysis object; its even physical staves {B2, B4, B6, B8} are read out, each returning an 18-sample × 10 ns = 180 ns waveform via a one-ended WLS-fibre/SiPM readout.

**Samples.** The two trigger configurations define two samples. **Sample I** = A·B trigger coincidence; **Sample II** = B trigger only (A ignored). The distinction between how the two samples relate in data and in MC is load-bearing and we keep it explicit: in the data, Samples I and II are **disjoint run sets** taken with different hardware trigger settings (Sample I = runs 44–57, Sample II = runs 58–63, 65; s22, s23); in the MC truth, the coincidence is applied as an inclusive per-event flag, so Sample I is a **subset** of Sample II (s21, s23). Every data–MC sample comparison inherits this subset-vs-disjoint asymmetry, and we report the exclusive MC variant (II \ I) alongside the inclusive one as a bracket.

**Selection anchor.** The entry condition for every downstream analysis is a single exactly reproducible selection: reading the `HRDv` waveform, using the even physical staves, defining the baseline as the median of samples 0–3, and requiring a baseline-subtracted amplitude A > 1000 ADC yields exactly **640,737** selected B-stave pulse records (delta = 0 vs the reference note; S00). This count is boundary-tested (strict > 1000, baseline-subtracted at selection, channels {0,2,4,6}) and is the one anchor the external review verified as implemented consistently across the builder, the selector, and the configuration. Amplitude is stored baseline-subtracted (net); a fraction of rows have amplitude below the hardware baseline, which is impossible for a raw peak and confirms the net convention (review C2; phase2_geometry). The true B2 net median is 5752 ADC.

**Uncertainty conventions.** Unless stated otherwise, σ₆₈ denotes the robust half-inter-quantile width (Q84 − Q16)/2, and quoted intervals are 95% bootstrap confidence intervals or LORO run-to-run spreads as noted at each number. AUC values are quoted to four decimals from the MV1 truth summary. MC truth is from GEANT4 11.2.2 / ROOT 6.32 (`hibeam_g4`), 1,000,000 primary events.

## 3. Methods

### 3.1 Reproduce-first discipline

Every study begins from the raw ROOT files and re-derives its inputs, gated on the exact 640,737-record selection anchor before any claim is registered. The distinction between the two candidate selectors (640,737 with the strict, baseline-subtracted cut vs 706,373 under a looser variant) is tracked explicitly (S00b/c) rather than silently absorbed, so that a one-line change in the selection cannot propagate unnoticed into a downstream number. Counts, checksums, and reproduce commands are recorded per study; the analysis reporting rules are fixed in a written standard that each study obeys.

### 3.2 Leakage controls

Any comparison that lets a flexible model win must be defended against leakage — the model learning a label that is a disguised function of its input. We apply three sentinels. **Target-shuffle** permutes labels to establish the null. **Leave-one-run-out (LORO)** trains on all but one beam run and evaluates on the held-out run, so a model cannot exploit run-specific idiosyncrasies. **Event-block shuffle** breaks within-event correlations. A win is registered only if it beats a *strong* conventional baseline on held-out runs with a confidence interval excluding zero **and** survives all three sentinels. Several tentative ML wins were rejected under LORO or event-block shuffle; we report those rejections as findings. The clearest illustration is a set of D_t/curvature PID classifiers that reached AUC ≈ 1.0 and were flagged as self-referential leakage rather than wins, because the label was computed from the input (S07b/e/g). We note two honest limits of our own controls, both surfaced by the external review: the implemented "event-block shuffle" is a global donor permutation rather than a strict time-block null, and several bootstraps iid-resampled linearly dependent pair residuals, making some CIs narrower than they should be. These caveats bound how far individual sub-0.3-ns claims can be pushed, and motivate the statistics-hardening pass in §3.5.

### 3.3 The MC digitizer and its known limits

The MC truth bridge requires GEANT4 energy deposits to be turned into sampled ADC waveforms comparable to data. This digitizer is the single most fragile component of the program, and we state its status plainly. The 2026-06 shared-library digitizer was found to be physically wrong on several counts (review C1, C5): the hit time cancelled exactly so transport smearing had no effect, the pulse kernel was differenced rather than integrated (producing a one-sample spike with a negative tail), and noise was seeded by event ID alone, giving 100% inter-channel noise correlation. The digitizer gain drifted across four sources (120 / 246 / 92 ADC/MeV). Consequently, the absolute energy scale is treated as unknown throughout: we report **gain ≈ 60–80 ADC/MeV, dominated by trigger and quenching modelling, with no precision value yet**. The earlier "calibrated" values v1 (≈246) and v2 (92 ± 28) are both retracted — v2 was computed on a folded |net − pedestal| variable of an already-baseline-subtracted amplitude and is unreproducible from any committed script (review I1, MV0). The 297 ADC/MeV appearing in the digitized pulse card is an explicit placeholder (the C2-corrected data-side anchor over trigger-unselected MC), not a measurement. Where absolute ADC would otherwise enter a comparison we defuse it: shape metrics are computed on median-scaled spectra and MC high-amplitude thresholds are quantile-matched, so the placeholder gain cancels to first order (s23). The zero-signal pedestal branch of the digitizer was validated on a 100,000-record zero-signal MC sample (MV7): the adaptive estimator (median of samples 0–3) reaches MAE 3.48 ADC and a learned ridge estimator 1.50 ADC — MC-level lower bounds only, since correlated noise, drift, and signal contamination are not modelled and the data has no true-pedestal sample (phase4, mc02_pulse_table).

### 3.4 The trigger mimic

The production GEANT4 geometry does not simulate the two-arm coincidence trigger that the data was actually recorded with. To compare like with like we apply a **truth-level trigger proxy**: an event is accepted into the Sample-I mimic if a charged particle enters the first A-arm layer and the first B-arm layer within 15 ns. This proxy is deliberately crude relative to the real trigger-paddle coincidence, and we treat the residual mismatch as a known limitation (§5.4). The recommended MC-side upgrade — scoring the trigger-bar volumes as sensitive detectors to emit a real per-event trigger flag — is identified but not yet run. The trigger mimic is the pivotal methodological device of the post-review program: it is what allows the stopping-depth discrepancy (§5.4) and the Sample-I enrichment (§5.3) to be diagnosed on the existing 1M-event sample without a new production.

### 3.5 Statistics hardening

Because ~230 adaptive studies were run on a single dataset, chance "wins" are expected and must be controlled. We ran a program-level Benjamini–Hochberg (BH) false-discovery-rate census over 1,948 delta-with-CI claims across 443 artifacts (stats01). Of the 17 scoreboard-bold ML wins, **11 survive BH, 0 fail, and 6 rest on prose or derived numbers with no machine-readable delta-CI**. BH survival is treated as necessary, not sufficient: the worked cautionary case is S03k, whose delta-CI claims survive BH yet were falsified by dedicated feature-leakage null grids (S03p/S03r) — multiplicity control cannot detect leakage. A confirmation run-partition (runs 64 and 12–30) is reserved for sub-0.3-ns timing claims and has not been spent. A single shared, tested estimators module replaces per-study bootstrap code going forward.

## 4. Results

We organise results by physics: timing (§4.1), pile-up (§4.2), PID and Sample-I/II enrichment (§4.3), stopping depth (§4.4), the early-peak anomaly class (§4.5), and the ML-vs-traditional verdicts (§4.6). Confidence labels follow: ✅ validated in data and consistent with MC; ⚠️ strong data result, MC pending or MC-only; 🔶 MC and data disagree beyond tolerance; ❌ apparent result rejected by a control.

### 4.1 Timing

**The analytic timewalk correction is the champion.** A constant-fraction discriminator (CFD20) pickoff gives a raw single-stave σ₆₈ near 1.85 ns; once a proper analytic amplitude-timewalk correction is applied, the conventional method reaches **σ₆₈ ≈ 1.49–1.55 ns** (LORO) and is very hard to beat (S02, S03a–d). ML residual correctors shave this only to ≈1.39–1.47 ns, and that gain is control-sensitive: it shrinks under LORO and fails event-block shuffle. The best *traditional* variant, adding pretrigger-proxy terms, reaches 1.343 ns and beats the corresponding ML residual (1.470 ns). One learned model (HGB on waveform+shape+stave features) reaches 1.107 ns in-fold and CI-beats the analytic comparator, but downstream substitution is **gated** pending a transfer/leakage audit — distinct from both "wins" and "rejected." Deep waveform networks (MLP, 1D-CNN) add nothing for timing here (P03). ⚠️/✅

**Resolution scales as 1/A, not 1/√A.** Measuring per-pair σ₆₈ against the minimum-pair amplitude, per sample, with residuals centred per (pair, run) before pooling (so cable-delay offsets are removed rather than folded into the width), the raw curves follow a 1/A scaling better than the naive 1/√A photostatistics expectation in the high-lever pairs: for B4–B6 the 1/A fit gives χ²/ndf = 0.32 (Sample I) and 0.87 (Sample II) versus 1.25 and 3.71 for 1/√A; the low-lever B6–B8 pair, dominated by a constant floor, ties between the two forms (s22). A 1/A law is the signature of an amplitude-linear timewalk rather than pure photon-counting jitter, and it is the physical justification for why an analytic amplitude correction, not a learned one, is the right tool.

**Downstream per-stave resolution reaches ≈0.85–1.1 ns at high amplitude.** Converting the pair widths to single-stave resolution as σ_pair/√2 (assuming independent, equal-variance stave errors) and cross-checking with a triangle decomposition, the downstream staves reach ≈0.85–1.1 ns at high amplitude; e.g. the B6–B8 pair reaches σ₆₈ = 0.90–0.94 ns above ≈2500 ADC after correction (s22). Two boundaries are stated inline. First, B2-containing pairs are saturation-flagged — 30–40% of selected B2 pulses exceed ≈7000 ADC, compressing both the CFD threshold and the timewalk feature — and are excluded from the headline per-stave claim. Second, the √2 conversion assumes independent stave errors; the inter-stave covariance is B2-/topology-dominated and the naive independence is imperfect, so the previously quoted 3-stave combination (≈0.54–0.56 ns) is carried only under review — its covariance-closure script was found numerically invalid and withdrawn (review I4). The clean single-stave precision therefore rests on the downstream staves B4/B6/B8, which should define any precision event-time estimate. ⚠️

**MC timing status.** The MC timing validation (MV4) was rerun honestly with a rising-edge-constrained CFD and a corrected functional form; the fitted timewalk coefficient is now physical (B = +39.6 ns·ADC) and the MC pair-equivalent σ₆₈ = 2.087 ± 0.009 ns sits between the data raw (2.993 ns) and corrected (1.50 ns) anchors. The verdict is **REVIEW**: the comparison is still merged-track MC versus per-stave data with unmatched selection, and a matched per-stave rerun awaits the unified digitizer. The earlier MV4 "PASS/TENSION" verdicts are withdrawn — they compared an ML-corrected data anchor against raw MC, mixed single-trace MC with pair-difference data (missing a √2), and assumed rather than measured σ_data (review P3). ⚠️

### 4.2 Pile-up

**The rate ceiling is a one-sided bound, R_max ≤ 3.05 MHz.** The earlier headline R_max ≈ 4.22 MHz assumed an effective live-time τ_eff = 90 ns. Direct measurement of the waveform live-time window finds all thresholds imply > 90 ns: the 10%-tail-crossing live-time is **124.79 ns** (bootstrap CI [123.33, 126.36] ns), ≈30% larger. Because the acquisition window heavily censors the tail (72.6% of pulses show positive inflation at the live-20 threshold), R_max = 0.380/τ_eff is a **one-sided upper bound**, not a two-sided value: censoring-aware estimators (Kaplan–Meier 151.6 ns, IPCW 179.1 ns) push τ_eff higher, giving **R_max ≤ 3.05 MHz, plausibly ≈2.1 MHz or lower** (S10). The note's 90 ns → 4.22 MHz figure is corrected, but as a ceiling. ⚠️

**The first independent MC live-time disagrees by +8%.** The 2026-06 MC validation of the pile-up ceiling (MV5) was retracted: its "MC τ_eff" was a hardcoded copy of the data value (review I2), so its claimed <0.01% agreement was the rounding error of one number compared with itself. The validation slot is now filled by the first genuinely independent MC live-time (MC03/S24): applying the same 10%-tail-crossing estimator to digitized MC single pulses gives **τ_eff = 134.99 ns [134.96, 135.01] vs data 124.79 ns (Δ = +10.2 ns, +8%, driven by B2 at 141.35 ns)**. This is an honest disagreement, not a closure: MC pulses are clean by construction while the data value includes real pathologies. 🔶

**Two-pulse recovery is a split verdict.** The earlier two-pulse comparison (S11a; failure rates 0.295 vs 0.168) is retired — its fit hypothesis grid coincided with the injection grid, its injected waveforms came from the fit's own templates, and its failure definitions differed between methods (review P8). The replacement is a truth-labelled benchmark (MC03/S24): continuous injected separations (exponential, inverse-CDF sampled), digitized real truth-hit pairs, one failure definition for both methods (missed detection or |Δt_rec − Δt_true| > 15 ns), and detection thresholds set by the same procedure with the headline read at a matched 80% coverage. The outcome is genuinely split: at matched 80% coverage the **traditional fit wins** (failure ≤ 0.0001 vs ML 0.0001–0.0002 across 0.5–3 MHz); at **full coverage ML wins** (0.011 vs 0.048); and on the common accepted subset the traditional Δt resolution is better (σ₆₈ = 0.64 ns vs ML 0.89 ns). We flag the residual circularity honestly: the fit template shares the digitizer kernel family with the generator, and the trigger phase is fixed, so absolute failure rates are optimistic — a data-side template-mismatch stress remains future work. ✅ (as an MC-truth benchmark)

**Current-dependent excess is real but heterogeneous.** The high-current pile-up excess has a CI excluding zero, but after matching on amplitude, baseline, and topology it concentrates in high-amplitude, large-baseline-lowering, broad-late pulses (matched excess ≈0.005–0.020 per event depending on stratum; S10c–f, S11b–d). Topology, not a learned "pile-up score," is the physics-facing rate handle; the ML/CWoLa treatment is monitoring/diagnostic only.

### 4.3 Particle identification and Sample-I/II enrichment

**Proton/deuteron PID is MC-closed at AUC = 0.986.** The data-only PID program (S07) could not by itself prove per-event species identity — classifiers reaching AUC ≈ 1.0 were self-referential leakage, and honest data PID sat near a ceiling it could not tie to a true label. The MC truth bridge closes it: on 400,369 truth tracks (150,130 protons, 146,842 deuterons; 37.5% and 36.7% of B-arm charged tracks), a histogram gradient-boosting classifier on truth features reaches **AUC = 0.9860** with purity 0.9644 at 90% efficiency, versus 0.9629 for logistic regression and 0.891 for a single ΔE cut (MV1). Data methods reach within 0.5% of this MC ceiling, i.e. the data carries essentially the same separating information once a true label exists. ✅

**The separation is kinematic and its energy scale is understood, if not absolute.** At truth level, deuterons deposit and stop early (layers 0–1) while protons penetrate (layers 4–7, proton fraction ≈0.89–0.90); after a momentum-unit fix (review C3), entry kinetic energies are MeV-scale (mean proton 99.4 MeV, deuteron 79.7 MeV) and total-deposit medians are proton 101.1 MeV, deuteron 73.4 MeV, with containment fractions 0.70 and 0.84 (MV2; the previously quoted 23/89 MeV were untraceable and are retracted). Deuterons stop early because pd-elastic kinematics gives them ≈105 MeV with roughly half a proton's range. This validates the *relative* range ordering; absolute per-event energy remains unreachable from data (§4.6). ⚠️ (geometry/trigger caveat: MV2 runs on the untriggered MC population.)

**Sample-I deuteron enrichment — confirmed at truth level and in data.** The coincidence trigger should preferentially tag the deuteron-into-B branch of the pd pair. This is confirmed on both sides. In MC truth (S21), the B2 deuteron fraction ratio Sample I / Sample II is **1.519 [1.510, 1.528]** (exclusive I vs II\I: 1.912 [1.898, 1.925]), and 91.2% of Sample-I events are deuteron-into-B / proton-into-A pairs — the direct signature of the correlated pd pair. In data (S23), the Sample-I B2 spectrum is harder: the high-amplitude fraction ratio f(A>5000)_I/II = **3.45 [3.41, 3.50]** (B2 median 6542 vs 3350 ADC). Applying the trigger mimic moves MC toward data (B2 median-scaled KS 0.192 → 0.131; occupancy χ² 624k → 20k), and the between-sample enrichment double ratio for B2 occupancy is **0.738 [0.733, 0.742] (z = −99 vs 1)** — MC under-predicts the contrast, consistent with the trigger-modelling deficit of §4.4. The direction of every effect matches; the residual gap is the known trigger/geometry limitation, so we claim the enrichment mechanism, not absolute agreement. ✅

### 4.4 Stopping depth: an unsimulated trigger, not missing material

The MC and data stave-occupancy profiles disagree sharply. The untriggered MC puts 47% of stopping pulses in B2 versus 87.6% in data, and overestimates deep-stave (B8) penetration by ≈10×, giving χ²/ndf = 68,269 (4 stave bins; MV3 v3). The 2026-06 narrative attributed this to missing upstream detector material. The post-review geometry audit **falsifies** that narrative and identifies the true cause (phase2_geometry).

A direct `TGeoManager` dump of the production geometry shows every named upstream component already present — CD₂ target, Mylar vacuum window, both arms' trigger scintillators, and the proto-TPC — with only ≈0.13 g/cm² of air genuinely absent (the world volume is vacuum). A material explanation of the B2 deficit would require degrading the 153 MeV elastic protons enough to stop in B2, i.e. **≥ 10.5 g/cm²** of upstream material; the physically available missing budget (air ≈0.13 + wrapping ≲0.5–0.8 g/cm²) is a factor ≥13 short. No amount of defensible material can move the MC profile to the data profile.

The cause is the unsimulated two-arm coincidence trigger. Requiring the truth-level A-arm coincidence collapses χ²/ndf from 68,269 to 3,141; adding an event basis, species-inclusive counting, and gain 60 reaches **625 — a 109× reduction** (best grid point 555; against the C2-corrected data profile, 88,542 → 1,061, 83×). The physics is transparent: when the deuteron goes into Stack B, its conjugate ≈85 MeV proton reaches Stack A and the coincidence fires; when the proton goes into Stack B, its conjugate ≈37 MeV deuteron dies in the TPC hull and there is no coincidence. The trigger therefore keeps deuteron-into-B (shallow, B2) events and vetoes deep-proton events; the untriggered MC contains both populations, which is the entire 68,269. Two rival hypotheses are closed in passing: the event-vs-track basis explains nothing (χ² slightly worse), and the alternative odd-layer stave mapping is disfavoured (both unread-bar variants are worse at every gain/trigger setting), so the `paired` mapping stands.

MV3 is accordingly re-graded **FAIL → TENSION**. No new GEANT4 production is required; the inter-stave-material pull request that the old narrative motivated should be closed rather than merged. The residual χ²/ndf ≈600–1,100 corresponds to ≲1% absolute per-stave deviations and belongs to the trigger-flag and digitizer work items, not to a geometry error. 🔶

### 4.5 The early-peak anomaly class: C12 recoils ruled out

Unsupervised analysis of the pulse shapes discovered, with no labels, a ≈4.4% class of early-peak / near-zero-area pulses (peak at sample ≈3, low amplitude; P02). The 2026-06 MC identification attributed this class to carbon-12 (C12) recoils. That identification is **ruled out** (MV6b). The retracted MV6 ran with the do-not-use gain 246, no Birks quenching (overstating C12 light by ≈10×), no amplitude threshold despite claiming one, and per-track whole-arm waveforms versus per-stave data pulses (review P5). The honest redo applies a physical per-hit Birks quenching law (kB = 0.0126 g MeV⁻¹ cm⁻², PSTAR/ASTAR-anchored dE/dx) and compares a quenched digitized table against its unquenched twin under the data taxonomy: **0 of 1,656 quenched C12-dominant records pass the A > 1000 selection at any gain hypothesis in 60–297** (unquenched, only 3 pass, at gain 297), and the MC early-peak fraction is 0.000% in every configuration versus 4.4% in data. The early-peak class therefore cannot be a species or scintillation effect; it must be instrumental (baseline/noise/bipolar artifacts) or trigger-phase-related — neither of which the MC models — and it is reopened as a **data-side** question. ✅ (the exclusion is MC-confirmed)

### 4.6 Machine learning versus traditional methods: honest verdicts

The pattern across the program is consistent and physically interpretable: **ML wins when the truth is independent of the input and the missing information lives in waveform shape; it ties or loses when an analytic physics model is already optimal, or when the apparent win is leakage.**

Clear ML wins, each with a CI excluding zero and surviving leakage controls: duplicate-readout amplitude/charge closure (res68 ≈ 0.003–0.009 vs 0.12–0.20 for peak/integral; P04), and artificial-saturation recovery (res68 ≈ 0.032–0.046 vs template 0.104–0.286, a 3–7× gain that degrades gracefully as clipping worsens; P07). Natural-saturation transfer carries a run-dependent timing-tail envelope and needs boundary audits before production. Two-pulse detection joins this list only at full coverage (§4.2).

Ties or losses for ML: timewalk correction (analytic wins, §4.1), pile-up rate (Poisson/topology analytic handle, §4.2), and conditional-template energy reconstruction, where an explicit analytic timewalk beats a learned conditional template on the primary metric (P10). On representation, an autoencoder beats PCA by 40–51% at latent dimension 2–4 but loses to PCA at dimension 8 (the small AE underfits), and for actual downstream tasks the learned latent does **not** robustly beat hand-crafted or PCA features once run-family and event-block sentinels are applied — the representation-superiority claim is **rejected** as the program's clearest disciplined falsification (P01, P02). ❌

Absolute per-event energy is structurally unreachable from data to the 10% target (propagated res68 ≈ 0.19–0.25; S14), because there is no per-event energy truth in the data; MC confirms the *limitation* by finding the physics-anchored Birks lookup to be the best held-out energy method, not superseded by neural or tree models (MV2). ✅ (the limitation is MC-confirmed)

## 5. Discussion

**The central advance is a disciplined characterisation, not a new device.** Two engineering-facing numbers stand: an analytic-limited same-particle pair timing resolution of σ₆₈ ≈ 1.49–1.55 ns (downstream per-stave ≈0.85–1.1 ns at high amplitude), and a corrected pile-up ceiling of R_max ≤ 3.05 MHz. The first is bounded by physics we understand — the 1/A resolution law is the fingerprint of amplitude-linear timewalk, which is exactly why an analytic amplitude correction is optimal and a learned corrector adds little. The second is deliberately reported as a one-sided bound because the acquisition window censors the pulse tail; quoting a two-sided ±1.5 ns τ_eff would have overstated what the data supports.

**What the ML results mean.** The task-by-task verdict is not "ML does not work" but a sharper statement about where information lives. When the label is independent of the input and the discriminating structure is in the waveform shape — recovering a clipped amplitude, closing a duplicate readout — a flexible model extracts it and wins by a wide margin. When an analytic model already saturates the available information (timewalk, Poisson pile-up rate), ML ties or loses; and when a label is a disguised function of the input, an unguarded ML pipeline manufactures a win that a leakage control then destroys. The program's most reusable methodological output is the demonstration that these three regimes are separable in practice, and that separating them requires held-out runs and shuffle sentinels rather than a single train/test split.

**Rival explanations, addressed.** The two most consequential data–MC discrepancies both had a tempting "detector is wrong" reading, and both turned out to be comparison artifacts. The stopping-depth discrepancy read as missing material; a direct geometry dump plus a range calculation shows the required material does not physically exist, and a trigger proxy reproduces the profile — the MC geometry is adequate, the MC *selection* was not. The pile-up τ_eff appeared MC-validated; the "validation" was a number compared with itself, and an honest independent measurement disagrees by +8%. In both cases the honest diagnosis distinguishes a comparison-level failure (trigger selection, event basis, species inclusion) from a model failure (geometry, code), and tests the distinction rather than asserting it. The C12 attribution of the early-peak class is a third rival explanation, now excluded by quenched MC, which relocates the question to the data side.

**Relation to the analysis method itself.** This manuscript is unusual in that its own headline numbers were corrected mid-course by an independent review that found asserted-without-code results, circular validations, and stripped caveats. We report the corrected state, and we regard the correction as part of the result. An autonomous, high-throughput analysis fleet can generate hundreds of studies, but the trustworthy subset is defined only by adversarial validation, exact reproduction, and a willingness to retract. The FDR census — 11 of 17 bold wins surviving, 6 resting on prose alone, with BH survival explicitly declared necessary but not sufficient — is the quantitative form of that posture.

## 6. Limitations and open questions

The following are stated as boundaries on the claims above, not as generic disclaimers.

- **Absolute energy scale is unknown.** The digitizer gain is ≈60–80 ADC/MeV with no precision value; all ADC-based comparisons are made scale-free (median-scaled shapes, quantile-matched thresholds). Retracted gain values (v1 246, v2 92 ± 28) must not be used; 297 ADC/MeV is a placeholder. Closing this requires a quenched, trigger-consistent gain re-scan (expected optimum ≈70–80) anchored on a geometry-robust observable.
- **MC timing is not yet a matched comparison.** MV4 remains REVIEW: merged-track MC versus per-stave data with unmatched selection. A matched per-stave rerun on the unified digitizer, with the data selection applied and σ_data measured, is required before an MC timing verdict can stand.
- **The MC live-time exceeds data by +8%** (134.99 vs 124.79 ns), driven by B2. MC pulses are clean by construction; a data-matched pathology model on the MC side and a per-stave comparison are the path to close it.
- **The residual stopping-depth tension (χ²/ndf ≈600–1,100) is open.** The truth-level trigger proxy is crude relative to the real paddle coincidence, the threshold model lacks Birks quenching, and data B2 carries pile-up. Scoring the trigger-bar volumes as sensitive detectors to emit a real per-event trigger flag is the identified next step; no new geometry production is required.
- **The 4.4% early-peak class is unexplained.** A species/scintillation origin is ruled out; the MC models neither trigger-phase jitter nor DAQ artifacts, so the mechanism (baseline/noise/bipolar, or trigger phase) must be pursued on the data side.
- **No true-pedestal sample exists in the data.** All pedestal validation is proxy-based (MV7 closes only the MC side); a forced/random-trigger capability in a future beam run would close it.
- **Inter-stave timing covariance is unmeasured.** The √2 per-stave conversion and the withdrawn 3-stave combination assume independent stave errors; the covariance is B2-/topology-dominated, so multi-stave precision claims below ≈1 ns are provisional and reserved for the unspent confirmation partition.
- **Statistical multiplicity is controlled but not eliminated.** BH survival is necessary, not sufficient; sub-0.3-ns timing claims (including the gated 1.107 ns) await confirmation on the reserved run-partition, and several bootstraps understate CI width by iid-resampling dependent residuals.

---

## 7. Conclusion

Using a two-arm scintillator range-telescope test beam, we characterised same-particle timing resolution and beam pile-up for a pulse that proves to be low-dimensional in shape and well described by analytic models. Timing is analytic-limited at σ₆₈ ≈ 1.49–1.55 ns for same-particle pairs, with a 1/A resolution law and downstream per-stave resolution reaching ≈0.85–1.1 ns at high amplitude; the pile-up rate ceiling is a data-driven one-sided bound R_max ≤ 3.05 MHz, revised down from a previously quoted 4.2 MHz, with the first independent MC live-time disagreeing by an honest +8%. Proton/deuteron identification is MC-closed at AUC = 0.986 with data methods within 0.5% of that ceiling, and the coincidence trigger's deuteron enrichment of Sample I is confirmed in both truth and data. A large MC–data stopping-depth discrepancy is caused by the unsimulated two-arm coincidence trigger rather than by missing detector material, and the carbon-recoil identification of a 4.4% early-peak anomaly class is ruled out. Machine learning helps decisively only where the missing information genuinely lives in waveform shape — saturation recovery and duplicate-readout closure — and ties or loses where an analytic physics model is already optimal or where an apparent win is leakage. Within the stated boundaries — unknown absolute energy scale, an unmatched MC timing comparison, and a residual trigger-modelling tension — these results establish a validated, honestly bounded baseline for the detector's timing and rate performance, and a template for how an adversarially validated, reproduce-first program separates real detector physics from comparison artifacts.
